# 198 Class Action Settlements That Pay Without a Receipt

Most people who ignore a settlement notice do so for one reason: they think they will need a receipt. A growing share of class action settlements do not ask for one. The Settlement Lookup index was updated on November 19, 2026, and it tracks 591 settlements in total. Of those, 198 pay without receipts or purchase records, and 144 are still open. That is a large enough group to be worth checking before you throw a notice away.

## What no proof actually means

A no-proof settlement defines the class by something the administrator can verify on its own, such as a data breach affecting a list of people or a purchase window tied to a retailer. You still have to fit the definition and file a claim, but you do not have to find an old box or a bank statement. The [no proof required settlements](https://settlements.resuhive.com/no-proof-required/) page lists all 198 and flags the 63 that close within 30 days, so you can see which ones need attention first.

## How to run a class action settlement lookup

Start with one of the three indexes. The [class action settlement lookup](https://settlements.resuhive.com/) homepage shows everything currently open. The [closing soon](https://settlements.resuhive.com/closing-soon/) filter narrows that to deadlines within a month. The [data breach category](https://settlements.resuhive.com/category/data-breach/) collects the largest group of no-proof claims. Search by company name if you recognise one, then open the entry and read the class definition, the deadline and the payout amount before you file.

## What filing costs

Nothing. The [how class action settlements work](https://settlements.resuhive.com/guides/how-class-action-settlements-work/) guide states plainly that a fee request is a scam, and that you can claim more than one settlement because each is separate. It also explains the difference between staying in the class, opting out and objecting. Payments shrink when more people claim, so the published amounts are often ceilings rather than guarantees.
